# Country conventions

Rules follow the **country of the job**. Offer them as suggestions with a one-line reason, ask, then apply (hard
rule 4). Where sources disagree, say so and default to the safe side: leaving a personal detail out rarely hurts,
adding one can. Sources are listed in `research/NOTES.md`.

**Paper:** Letter in the US, Canada, Mexico, the Philippines and parts of Latin America. A4 almost everywhere else.

| Country | Length | Photo | Personal details | Also |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | 1 page early career, 2 for long careers | No | Leave out birth date, age, marital status, nationality. Anti-discrimination law binds employers, so they prefer not to see them | "Resume". Work authorization is a common knockout question. A graduation year more than ~15 years back is optional |
| **Canada** | 1–2 pages | No ("not the norm", Job Bank) | Never age; nothing beyond name and contact | Newcomers often over-share: trimming is the main fix |
| **United Kingdom** | **2 pages is the norm**, even for graduates and mid-level | No | Leave out birth date and marital status (equality law). City is enough, no full address | "CV". British spelling (organise) |
| **Australia** | 2–3 pages, often up to 4 for long careers | No | Leave out birth date, marital status, gender, licence unless the role needs it | **Referees:** usually asked for later. "Referees available on request" or names without contact details are fine; don't put referees' phone numbers on a file uploaded to job boards without their OK, unless the posting asks (e.g. government roles). Australian spelling |
| **Israel** | 1 page preferred, 2 max | No, except client-facing roles or when asked | Birth date optional, safe to leave out | **Military service** is a normal section: role and years (rank optional). **Never** classified unit names or operational details. For jobs abroad, translate military roles into civilian terms ("Network operations technician, 3 years"). Language follows the posting (tech is often English); keep a Hebrew and an English version |
| **Germany** | Usually 2 pages | Common but optional; international companies accept none | Birth date and nationality often included, optional | Exact MM/YYYY dates, and a **gapless timeline**: explain gaps over 2–3 months in one line (study, travel, job search). Signature and date at the end are mostly outdated, except in traditional firms and the public sector. "Lebenslauf" |
| **France** | 1 page for early career; 2 is common from ~5–10 years' experience | Customary but optional | Age and marital status optional, more and more left out | The anonymous CV ("CV anonyme") was made optional in 2015 and is rarely used |
| **Netherlands** | 1–2 pages, short and direct | Follow the posting; never an ID photo | Name, city, email, phone are enough. Leaving out birth date, photo and nationality costs nothing. **Never the BSN** | Short profile at the top. Language levels as CEFR (B2, C1). A short cover letter is standard |
| **Poland** | 1–2 pages | Often still expected; declining in IT and international firms | Optional | **RODO (GDPR) consent clause** at the bottom is widely expected. It matters most when the CV holds more than the labour code's basic data (a photo, hobbies). Lawyers disagree on whether it's strictly required. Use the employer's wording if the posting gives one, else the wording below. Never the old 1997 formula |
| **India** | 1 page for freshers, 1–2 for experienced | Usually not for IT or private companies | **Private sector:** leave out father's name, birth date, marital status, religion, caste and the "declaration" line. **Government/PSU:** these are still asked (age limits), so keep a full "biodata" version | State the **notice period** (often a screening question in IT). Two versions are normal |

**Any other country:** use the safe default (no photo or personal details, 1–2 pages, the country's paper size) and
tell the candidate this skill has no specific rules for that country. Don't make up local norms. Japan's rirekisho,
for example, is a fixed form: point them to it rather than imitating it.

## Polish RODO clause (when the posting gives no wording)

> Wyrażam zgodę na przetwarzanie moich danych osobowych dla potrzeb niezbędnych do realizacji procesu rekrutacji
> zgodnie z Rozporządzeniem Parlamentu Europejskiego i Rady (UE) 2016/679 z dnia 27 kwietnia 2016 r. (RODO).

Put it in `footer_note` when rebuilding. Add a sentence about future recruitment only if the candidate wants that.

## Applying across countries

When the resume follows one country's style and the job is in another (e.g. a German CV for a US job), list the
differences as one short checklist:
- photo
- personal details
- length
- paper size
- date style
- what the document is called

Apply what they approve.
