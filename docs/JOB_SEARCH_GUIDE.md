# Job search guide

The thinking behind the search settings, in our own words. Numbers from blogs and vendors vary a lot; read them as
direction, not facts. Sources at the end.

## Quality over quantity

- **A tailored application beats a mass one.** Guides that track outcomes agree that a smaller number of matched,
  tailored applications gets more interviews per hour than applying to everything. That's why the agent caps the
  jobs per run (`max_new_jobs_per_run`) and keeps only good matches (`min_match_score`).
- **A steady pace:** suggestions range from about 5–10 careful applications a week to 15–30 lighter ones. Daily
  short runs with 5–8 jobs keep the list small enough to review.
- **Apply early:** many postings get most applicants in the first days. Search often with a short look-back window.

## Referrals and networking

- **A referral is the strongest single channel.** Commonly quoted figures put referred candidates' interview rate
  many times above cold applications. Ask former colleagues, classmates and alumni who work at companies you target;
  a short, specific message works best.
- **Your profile is part of the search:** keep LinkedIn consistent with your resume (title, dates, skills).
- The agent never messages people for you unless you switch on that apply method for a specific job.

## Follow-up

- Track every application with its date and status (the Applications tab does this).
- If you hear nothing, one polite follow-up after about 7–10 working days is common advice; a second one a week
  later at most.
- Use what you learn: rejections and silence after many applications usually point to the resume or the targeting,
  not to luck. The *Check my resume* button and the match score help find which.

## Where to look

- Your country's main boards, LinkedIn, and company career pages (many jobs are only there). The setup wizard
  recommends sites for 12 countries; with an AI set up it can suggest more for any country and field.
- Field communities: meetups, Slack/Discord/Telegram groups, newsletters with job boards.
- Watch for ghost jobs and scams (see the resume guide and the agent's rules).
