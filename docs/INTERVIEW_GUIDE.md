# Interview guide

The general part of every interview prep the agent writes. For each interview it adds a page for that company and
role (see *Prepare* in the control panel), built from the posting, your resume and guides for your field. Summarized
in our own words; sources at the end.

## Before

- **Ask about the format** when they invite you: how long, with whom, technical or not, live coding or a home task.
- **Research the company:** what they sell, who uses it, recent news, the team you'd join. Their own careers or
  engineering pages often say how they interview.
- **Re-read the posting and your resume.** Every line of your resume is fair game; be ready to talk about each one.
- **Prepare 4–6 stories** from your experience, projects or studies in the **STAR** shape: the *Situation*, the
  *Task* you had, the *Action you took* (your part, not the team's), and the *Result* (with a number if you have one).
  One story can answer several questions: a hard bug, a deadline, a conflict, something you learned fast, a mistake.
- **Practice out loud**, ideally a mock interview with a friend. For technical roles, solve practice problems while
  explaining your thinking.
- **Prepare 3 questions to ask them** about the team, the first months, how success is measured.

## During

- **Think out loud** in technical questions: restate the problem, ask what's unclear, start simple, then improve.
- **It's fine to say “I don't know”**, followed by how you would find out. Guessing confidently is worse.
- **Keep answers focused:** about two minutes for a story; ask if they want more detail.
- **Be specific:** “I cut the build from 12 to 4 minutes by caching dependencies” beats “I improved the CI”.
- **Ask to repeat** a question if you lost it.

## Don't

- Don't invent experience or claim a skill you can't discuss: follow-up questions will find it.
- Don't speak badly about a former employer or team.
- Don't read answers from a screen or search for answers during a call (looking up an API name in a coding task,
  when allowed, is different).
- Don't bring up salary first in an early round unless they ask; have your range ready (`screening_answers`).

## After

- A short thank-you email the same day: one line about something you discussed.
- Write down the questions you got and what you'd answer better. The agent adds them to the next prep.
- If you hear nothing within the time they gave, one polite follow-up is fine.

## By field (what the agent adds for your interview)

| Field | Expect | Prepare |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Software development | Coding task, data structures basics, a project deep-dive, sometimes a small system design | Walk through one project end to end: why each choice, what you'd change |
| DevOps / Cloud | Linux, networking basics, CI/CD, containers, a cloud service you used, troubleshooting scenarios | Explain a pipeline or deployment you built; how you'd debug a failing service |
| Data / Analytics | SQL live, a case question, reading a chart, explaining a metric | Write joins and group-bys by hand; tell a story from a dashboard you built |
| QA | Test cases for a feature, bug reports, automation basics | Write test cases for something everyday (a login page) out loud |
| Design | Portfolio walkthrough, a design critique, process questions | Two case studies: problem, research, iterations, result |
| Product / Sales / Marketing | Case or role-play, metrics, prioritization | One example with a number; a 30-60-90 day idea for the role |
